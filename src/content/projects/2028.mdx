- **Live:** [hayashi.in/2028](/2028/)

Nobody has formally entered the 2028 race yet, but the same names come up again and again. I
wanted one page that puts them side by side: what the betting markets think, what the early
polls say, how likely each person is to actually run, and what they have done or said on the
issues I care about.

## What's on the page

The top table lists nine possible candidates, Republicans and Democrats, sorted by their odds of
winning their party's nomination. The odds are an average of Polymarket and Kalshi, taken from
[PredictMarketCap's comparison](https://predictmarketcap.com/elections/2028). Markets don't
price the popular vote per candidate, so "win presidency" is the closest measure. Next to that
is the latest primary poll I could find for each person.

The last column is a row of ballot ovals, one to five filled, for how likely the person is to
run. This one is my own read of public statements, not a market price, and the page says so.
Someone who says they'll decide after the midterms and already has a political team gets five.
Someone who says they have no interest gets two, even if the markets like them.

Below the table you pick an issue and see every candidate's position on it:

- Wealth inequality
- Campaign finance
- AI
- Climate and environment
- Financial regulation
- Corporate tax
- Education and science

## Tagging the evidence

Most of these people have no 2028 platform, so a flat list of positions would mix a bill
someone signed last year with a line from a campaign six years ago. Each entry carries a tag
for what it rests on:

- **2026 proposal:** something proposed or said this year, the closest thing to a platform.
- **Record:** votes, laws signed, or actions in office.
- **Past campaign:** a position from a 2020 or 2024 run, which may have moved since.
- **No clear position:** nothing specific found. That means unknown, not opposed.

The gaps turned out to be informative. On campaign finance, several candidates have nothing on
record at all, while for one of them it's the signature issue. On AI, the candidates split
along lines that don't follow party.

## Who's not on it

I only list people with real signals: market odds, poll numbers, or public talk about running. I
looked into adding Jack Smith, the former special counsel. He isn't in any 2028 market or poll I
could find, he's registered as an independent, and he has no public positions on these issues.
Adding him would have made the page imply a candidacy that doesn't exist, so he's out for now.

## How it's built

It's a single static HTML file with no build step and no framework. The candidates and issues
are two JavaScript arrays at the bottom of the file, and the page renders the table and the
issue view from them. Adding an issue means adding one entry to the list of issues and a
matching key to each candidate. Anyone missing that key shows as "No clear position"
automatically.

Every figure on the page is a snapshot dated September 27, 2026. The numbers will move a lot
after the November 2026 midterms, when announcements usually start.
