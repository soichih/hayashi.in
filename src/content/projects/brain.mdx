A little side project: my own T1-weighted anatomical brain scan, viewable right in the
browser using [Papaya](https://github.com/rii-mango/Papaya), a self-contained JavaScript
medical image viewer. No installs, no server-side processing — the NIfTI volume is fetched
and rendered entirely client-side, with synchronized axial, sagittal, and coronal views.

It's a fun way to poke around my own anatomy, and a nod to years spent building
neuroimaging tools at [brainlife.io](/projects/brainlife/) — this time pointed at my own
head instead of someone else's dataset.

To make it more than gray levels to eyeball, I also ran FreeSurfer's `recon-all` 8.2.0
on the scan (in Docker, on my home server) and baked the resulting `aparc+aseg`
parcellation — roughly a hundred labeled cortical and subcortical structures — into an
RGB overlay you can toggle on top of the raw anatomy.

The scan has been defaced (brain-extracted) before publishing, so no facial features are
visible in the volume. It's for personal curiosity, not clinical use.
