The world has evil people and predators, and they do try to hurt us. The tempting response is to become the strongest and meanest person around, so we can beat the people we fear.

That is not what made us who we are. We are not the strongest animal, and we ended up at the top anyway. What got us there is our ability to see each other's weaknesses and help where we can, to forgive flaws, and to find ways around our shortcomings. That includes standing up to bullies and enemies. We don't do it alone. We take on the risk together, and nobody is expected to carry it by themselves. We can picture a better tomorrow for all of us and build it together.

We have found a child who lived years with a severe skull deformity [1](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0900965106 "Gracia et al., PNAS 2009. A Middle Pleistocene child from Atapuerca, Spain, survived years with a severe congenital skull deformity. The authors say it may have needed extra care."), an adult who outlived nearly every tooth [2](https://www.nature.com/articles/434717b "Lordkipanidze et al., Nature 2005. A 1.77-million-year-old skull from Dmanisi, Georgia, belonged to someone who had lost all but one tooth several years before death."), and Neanderthals who recovered from serious injuries [3](https://doi.org/10.1080/00438243.2018.1433060 "Spikins et al., World Archaeology 2018. A review arguing that healed injuries show widespread Neanderthal healthcare within strong pro-social bonds."). Not everyone agrees what that means [4](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.1330840402 "Dettwyler, American Journal of Physical Anthropology 1991. The standard caution: skeletal evidence of impairment does not show the motives or attitudes of the community."). Hunter-gatherer bands keep the powerful in check together [5](https://doi.org/10.1086/204166 "Boehm et al., Current Anthropology 1993. A cross-cultural survey of how the rank and file of egalitarian bands keep individuals from dominating."). Early humans needed each other for food [6](https://sites.duke.edu/tomasellolabduke/files/2016/09/Tomasello_etal_2012.pdf "Tomasello et al., Current Anthropology 2012. Proposes that early humans became obligate collaborative foragers, so each had a direct interest in their partners' well-being.") and for raising children [7](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674060326 "Hrdy, Mothers and Others, 2009. Argues that humans descend from cooperative breeders, where children were raised by many people, not only their mothers.").

I wrote about the other half of this in [The obligation of the fortunate](/blog/the-obligation-of-the-fortunate/).

Sometimes we fail. We let a bully go after one person, or we let misfortune hit someone and we look away. Sometimes we can't forgive a shortcoming and we withhold grace. That is part of how we got here too, because a failure we remember teaches us something. A failure we forget teaches nothing.

We never stop. We make progress over time, and we must not give up on each other.

## Sources & Further Reading

1. **Craniosynostosis in the Middle Pleistocene human Cranium 14 from the Sima de los Huesos, Atapuerca, Spain** - Gracia et al., *PNAS* 106(16) (2009). Reports a child with a severe congenital skull deformity and discusses whether it needed extra care to survive.
   https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0900965106

2. **The earliest toothless hominin skull** - Lordkipanidze et al., *Nature* 434, 717-718 (2005). Describes the Dmanisi skull that had lost all but one tooth well before death.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/434717b

3. **Calculated or caring? Neanderthal healthcare in social context** - Spikins, Needham, Tilley and Hitchens, *World Archaeology* 50(3) (2018). Argues Neanderthal healthcare was widespread and part of strong pro-social bonds.
   https://doi.org/10.1080/00438243.2018.1433060

4. **Can paleopathology provide evidence for "compassion"?** - Dettwyler, *American Journal of Physical Anthropology* 84(4) (1991). The standard caution: skeletal evidence of impairment does not show the motives or attitudes of the community.
   https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.1330840402

5. **Egalitarian Behavior and Reverse Dominance Hierarchy** - Boehm et al., *Current Anthropology* 34(3) (1993). A cross-cultural survey of how the rank and file of egalitarian bands keep individuals from dominating.
   https://doi.org/10.1086/204166

6. **Two Key Steps in the Evolution of Human Cooperation: The Interdependence Hypothesis** - Tomasello, Melis, Tennie, Wyman and Herrmann, *Current Anthropology* 53(6) (2012). Proposes that obligate collaborative foraging made early humans interdependent.
   https://sites.duke.edu/tomasellolabduke/files/2016/09/Tomasello_etal_2012.pdf

7. **Mothers and Others: The Evolutionary Origins of Mutual Understanding** - Sarah Blaffer Hrdy, Harvard University Press (2009). Argues humans descend from cooperative breeders.
   https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674060326
