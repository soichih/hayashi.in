Your ballot

Every voter in the county gets the statewide offices, Congress, the county offices and the yes/no questions. Your State House district, county council district, township, school board and a few other items depend on your precinct.

Don't know your precinct? Look it up at indianavoters.in.gov. It shows on your voter registration. This page doesn't store what you pick.

How to read this

What the evidence tags mean
  • In their words: the candidate's own answer to a voter survey, debate or interview this year.
  • Record: votes cast or actions taken in office.
  • Campaign site: what the candidate's campaign says they will do.
  • Reporting: facts from news coverage, such as background, fundraising and endorsements.
Things the ballot doesn't tell you
  • A straight-party vote doesn't count for township board seats, school board seats, the judge retention questions or the public questions. Mark those separately.
  • Declared write-in candidates are not printed on the ballot. To vote for one, write the name on the write-in line and mark the box next to it. In Monroe County the declared write-ins are Floyd Michael Taylor (Independent) for Congress, Joe Davis (Independent) for Circuit Court Clerk, and Andrew Delano (filed as "National Socialist American Workers'") for Secretary of State. A write-in for anyone who didn't file is not counted.
  • There is no U.S. Senate, governor or state senate race on any Monroe County ballot this year.
  • "Uncontested" means one name for one seat, or exactly as many names as seats. Those candidates win with a single vote, but you can still vote for them or leave the race blank.
Where this comes from

The list of races, candidates and questions per precinct comes from the county election board's official sample ballots (the "2026 General Election Sample Ballots" folder linked from the Monroe County Public Library's voting page), dated September 18, 2026, cross-checked with the Secretary of State's candidate list as republished by The Indiana Citizen. Early voting dates are from the county's early voting schedule. Candidate positions come from the WFYI and Louisville Public Media 2026 voter guides, local reporting and the candidates' own sites.
